The request

A city analyst asks: how long do 311 cases near campus take to close, and does it depend on the type of request?

The data is data/hw3_campus_edge_311_2025.csv in your course folder, the file from HW3. One row is one case. The columns you need are case_status, case_request, date_opened, and date_closed.

What to do

  1. Keep the cases that are closed and have a closing date. Count them.
  2. For those cases, compute the days from opening to closing. Find the median and the mean.
  3. Among request types with at least 30 of those cases, find the type with the highest median.
  4. Write a note of at most five sentences for the analyst.

Rules

  • Set a timer for 90 minutes and stop when it ends.
  • Start from a new, empty notebook in the top folder of your course folder.
  • Use pandas or SQL.
  • Use documentation and your own notes. Do not use an AI assistant.

Check your numbers

Check your note

Read your note against this list.

  • It says which cases were counted, and out of how many.
  • It gives the median, and explains why the mean is so much higher.
  • It names the slowest request type with its number of cases.
  • It says which cases were left out and how that changes the answer.
  • It names one more limit of the data.
A model note

Of the 1,896 cases opened near campus in 2025, 1,636 are closed and have a closing date; this note is about those. Half of them closed within one day, but the mean is 20.1 days, because a small number of cases took months. Among request types with at least 30 closed cases, tree pruning is slowest, with a median of 16.5 days over 54 cases. The 260 cases without a usable closing date, most of them still open, are left out, and they are likely to be slow ones, so these numbers understate how long cases take. The city’s portal also removes cases with no activity for about a year, so cases from early 2025 are underrepresented; I would rerun this on a dated copy of the data before comparing request types.

Model code 
import pandas as pd

cases = pd.read_csv(
    "data/hw3_campus_edge_311_2025.csv",
    dtype={"case_id": str},
    parse_dates=["date_opened", "date_closed"],
)

# Closed cases with a closing date.
# One Closed case has no date. Two cases have a date but are not Closed.
closed = cases[(cases["case_status"] == "Closed") & cases["date_closed"].notna()].copy()
closed["days"] = (closed["date_closed"] - closed["date_opened"]).dt.days

print(len(closed), closed["days"].median(), round(closed["days"].mean(), 1))

by_type = closed.groupby("case_request")["days"].agg(["size", "median"])
by_type[by_type["size"] >= 30].sort_values("median", ascending=False).head()
